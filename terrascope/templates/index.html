☂ MONSOON MIRROR

How has Indian monsoon rainfall changed since 1981, and do different regions behave differently?

Monsoon Mirror is a preconfigured investigation inside TerraScope. It uses the same engine as every other analysis, so nothing here is special-cased.

VariableCorrected precipitation (PRECTOTCORR)
DatasetNASA POWER monthly API, MERRA-2 based
SeasonJune–September, rainfall total per year (mm)
Period1981 – latest complete year
RegionsNorthwest, Western, Central, Northeast, Southern India (6 sample points each)
TestsSen’s slope, Mann–Kendall (autocorrelation-aware), Benjamini–Hochberg FDR

No result is assumed in advance. Whatever the data shows is what gets reported, including “no significant trend detected”.

⚙ HOW IT WORKS

  1. Fetch. Monthly values come from the NASA POWER API (MERRA-2 based), one request per location, cached locally.
  2. Check. A season-year with any missing month becomes “missing”, never zero. At least 10 valid years are needed; below 20 you get a short-record warning.
  3. Aggregate. Rainfall: monthly mm/day × days in month, summed over the season. Other variables: day-weighted seasonal mean.
  4. Estimate. Sen’s slope gives the trend size (robust to outliers) with a 95% confidence interval. Ordinary linear regression is shown for comparison.
  5. Test. Mann–Kendall tests for a monotonic trend. If lag-1 autocorrelation is significant and positive, the Hamed–Rao modified version is used, because plain Mann–Kendall over-reports trends in that case.
  6. Correct. Many locations are tested at once, so some would look “significant” by luck. Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted values (q) control that, and the map uses q, not raw p.
  7. Compare. Regions with significant trends in opposite directions are flagged.

⚠ READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING A RESULT

  • MERRA-2 is a reanalysis (model + observations), not a rain-gauge record. Its rainfall is corrected over land but is still an estimate.
  • Each India zone is a set of sample points, not a full wall-to-wall grid. Zone values are the plain average of its points.
  • “No statistically significant trend detected” does not mean “no change”. The record may simply be too short or too noisy to be sure.
  • A trend shows what changed, not why. Causes need separate evidence.
  • Results depend on the chosen period and season.
